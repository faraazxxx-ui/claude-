## How to Use These Numbers in Mediation

**Open with Tier 1, not Tier 3.** The first number the mediator hears from us should be one we can hand over with receipts: the $1.22M documented core, wire ledger and hash chain included — which is exactly why Open Factual Flag 4 must be cleared first: until the executed Florida contract is in counsel's hands, the largest Tier 1 line has no receipt to hand over. That purchase of credibility is what lets every later number land. The apex substrate's own methodology note stands: *no orphan numbers in any filing*.

**Argue the case in Tier 2.** The working demand conversation lives in the $4.3M–$16.6M band, and the way to move within it is to *strengthen components*, not inflate them: get the vocational/economic expert engaged (front pay is $0 without one — *Tarpley*), get psychiatric treatment records (distress is garden-variety ~$100K without them), get the amendment filed (NYSHRL and FMLA carry the distress, punitive, doubling, and fee exposure that Title VI cannot). Each condition satisfied ratchets the defensible mid upward — that is the honest version of escalation.

**Deploy Tier 3 once, correctly.** The ~$100M figure is spoken to the mediator (ideally in caucus) as *their client's tail risk*, with the seven assumptions listed: "for this to happen, all seven break our way — but your carrier prices tails." Never present it as our valuation. Paired with the fee-shifting clock (defendants' exposure compounds monthly through the November deadline) it does its work without costing us anything.

**Trade money for the must-haves early and visibly.** The record correction, board-safe letters, and USCIS stipulation cost UHSH almost nothing and are worth more to Dr. Rahman than the last several million dollars of any demand. Signaling genuine flexibility on dollars in exchange for complete non-monetary relief also *proves* the theme — accountability and sustainable change, not punishment — which is both true and the most disarming posture available against a non-profit community hospital defendant.

**Know the floor and say it internally only.** Below Tier 1 plus the must-haves, the BATNA is trial: liability paths are multiple, fee-shifting is asymmetric, and individual defendants (Graziadio FMLA exposure; §296(6) aiding-and-abetting) face personal, largely uninsurable punitive risk that grows with every deposition. That is leverage we hold by *being willing to try the case* — never by threatening numbers we cannot defend.
