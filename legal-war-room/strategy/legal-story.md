## ONE-PAGE COLD OPEN

He came to this country to be a doctor, and he became a good one — good enough that when his own hospital tried to put him on probation, the current head of medical education wrote that it was "very inhumane for you to put this kid on probation... he's a good doctor" (00:26:23). ## THE LEGAL STORY — THEORY OF THE CASE (~1,200 words)

**1. A gifted immigrant physician the system needed — and used.**
Faraaz Rahman was a resident at UHS, the Southern Tier's largest health system (~$1.16B FY2024 revenue, ASSERTED [unverified — ProPublica 990 data, confirm with counsel]; r4-Q1). He trained through a serious cardiac condition — Brugada syndrome with an implanted ICD — which is a qualifying disability under the post-ADAAA framework regardless of the device's ameliorative effect (42 U.S.C. § 12102(4)(E)(i) [unverified — confirm with counsel]; r3-Q3). He was, by his own program's later admission, "a good doctor" (00:26:23). He was also, structurally, vulnerable: a non-citizen whose career, immigration status, and health insurance all ran through one employer.

**2. Protected complaints, institutional indifference.**
In 2021 and 2022 he filed official HR complaints about verbal abuse by Dr. Awais Ahmed, from his UHS email (01:13:37). "We went to HR, we filed the complaint, you did nothing" (00:08:02). Instead of separating them, UHS forced him to keep working under Ahmed — "that's the time I actually broke in my life" (00:11:46) — and Ahmed retaliated in kind: zero patients for a week, no eye contact, a mocking fellow (01:14:42). A senior resident begged him by text not to complain, for fear of his own cardiology match (01:15:55). The 2022 first attempt to force him out — a 6:00 a.m. meeting he was told to attend alone (00:19:00) — collapsed when he refused to sign a probation (00:35:01) and answered his file with a ten-page, line-by-line rebuttal sent to the entire GME (00:37:46). This history matters doctrinally: it is the protected-activity predicate for retaliation, and it defeats the "academic judgment" characterization before it starts.

**3. Thirteen days.**
On April 1, by his account, he signed the Florida contract — $350,000 plus $70,000 bonus, three years (DOCUMENTED-PENDING / ASSERTED; 00:13:03 — but see OPEN FACTUAL FLAG 2: the 00:14:21 canon has him with an offer he "had never signed"). On April 14 — thirteen days later — UHS placed him on probation *unilaterally and in secret* (00:14:21), on paperwork bearing what he alleges is a forged signature, documents UHS still holds (00:56:56); Dr. Ali was later "made to step down" over it, yet three years on the underlying decision stands unreversed (00:56:56). The head of medical education himself did not know, and called it "very inhumane" (00:26:23). The same day, Rahman invoked FMLA — protected activity under the motivating-factor standard of *Woods v. START Treatment & Recovery Centers*, 864 F.3d 158 (2d Cir. 2017) (CL cluster 4409966), with temporal proximity alone carrying prima facie causation under *Donnelly v. Greenburgh Central School District No. 7*, 691 F.3d 134 (2d Cir. 2012) (CL cluster 806387). In May the Vermont offer vanished (00:13:03). Escalation is the client's own framing and the evidence's shape: probation failed because the contract predated it; firing was blocked by FMLA; "so they had me arrested on... basically a false charge" (00:14:21, 00:15:52).

**4. The arrest that answered itself.**
Police were waiting at his car as he went to write his FMLA document (00:55:54), on the day he was scheduled to fly down for the Florida interview (00:14:21) — April 14 per the canon (00:42:37; see OPEN FACTUAL FLAG 3). The police call came from Guthrie — an institution, not the complainant (00:40:05). Guthrie's lawyers called UHS's lawyers within the hour; administrative leave came "straight from their legal team to our legal team" (00:42:37, 00:47:06). Wanted posters went up (00:42:37) and circulated into UHS (01:08:55). And then — the beat every audience must hear — **within about 24 hours both institutions reversed everything**: leave lifted, all investigations dropped, come back to work (00:43:40). UHS's own rules made arrest-on-probation instantly fireable; corporate counsel decided within an hour not to fire him (00:50:15), while comparator Ahmed went from administrative leave to fired in 3–6 hours (00:51:14). They reinstated him, graduated him (00:45:36) — conduct near-contemporaneously inconsistent with belief in guilt, which is the malice and no-probable-cause showing *Lowth v. Town of Cheektowaga*, 82 F.3d 563 (2d Cir. 1996) (CL cluster 717472) and *Manganiello v. City of New York*, 612 F.3d 149 (2d Cir. 2010) (CL cluster 2522805) let a jury infer. Yet UHS then fed the DA his academic file — the probation record he was simultaneously told he was never on (00:05:23) — and cut his email (00:51:14). Never produced: the third-party CCTV both legal teams apparently viewed before dropping everything (00:50:15). Under *Present v. Avon Products* (CL cluster 6162502) and *Rothstein v. Carriere*, 373 F.3d 275 (2d Cir. 2004) (CL cluster 786706), knowingly supplying false or incomplete material that induces and sustains a prosecution is initiation; the private defendants have no immunity (*Wyatt v. Cole*, CL cluster 112733; *Dennis v. Sparks*, CL cluster 110353).

**5. Total exoneration, persistent record.**
He refused an ACD because USCIS treats diversion as implied guilt (01:03:33), endured pre-trial supervision — weekly probation-officer check-ins, drug testing, travel restrictions (00:53:36) — and in September 2025 was found **NOT GUILTY**; the prosecution "was terminated in your favor" (01:02:46), satisfying favorable termination beyond argument under *Thompson v. Clark*, 596 U.S. 36 (2022) (CL opinion 6329458), with accrual at acquittal under *McDonough v. Smith*, 588 U.S. 109 (2019) (CL cluster 4631414). But acquittal repairs nothing the defendants broke: the licensing board asks "have you ever been arrested" (01:01:30); three immigration attorneys call the arrest record a serious USCIS problem (01:02:46); the license remains blocked (00:33:46); three years of medicine lost (01:03:33); ~$1M family drain (ESTIMABLE, 00:32:39, 00:28:43); a heart patient off his $4,000 medication (ASSERTED, 00:27:22) with an unchecked ICD (00:32:39).

**6. Why this suit — and why this ask.**
The client's true first-order objective is not a number: it is a **clean academic record, an open licensure path, and a USCIS-safe narrative**. Only this lawsuit can deliver those — through discovery of the complete academic file UHS refused to hand over even when the family asked in person and security was called on them (00:05:23), and through equitable relief (record correction, expungement of the probation, certification) that survives *Cummings v. Premier Rehab Keller*, 596 U.S. 212 (2022) (CL cluster 6463720) untouched. The pattern evidence — the Iranian resident hounded out by solicited complaints and unit-by-unit posters (00:18:03), the Indian resident fired six months from graduation over resignation-threat letters from her own colleagues (00:19:00), two more fired Indian physicians (00:59:30), while defendants Ali and Rehman each left roles over family-hiring conflicts of interest (00:09:26, 00:10:26) — makes the systemic point: this was not one resident's misfortune but a way of operating. That is why the closing frame is the ACGME complaint and structural relief (01:05:45): **accountability and sustainable change, not punishment**. We are on the right side of this conversation, and every element of the record — the 13 days, the 24 hours, the not-guilty verdict, the still-blocked license — keeps us there.
