A physician completing an internal medicine residency was accused by a woman he had been seeing, on a complaint filed the day after her parents discovered the relationship. He was arrested, prosecuted for seventeen months, and acquitted at trial. During the prosecution he could not obtain licensure or employment, and a signed $300,000 employment contract became unattainable. He held an implanted cardiac defibrillator throughout.

The evidence is unusual in three respects, and each of them is documentary rather than testimonial:

  • An audio recording containing an admission of fabrication — words to the effect of "We knew it is false. We framed him."
  • Video of the complainant's police statement showing her being coached — apparently by the officer present, and separately by a person on the telephone during the statement itself.
  • A photograph of the complainant at the accused's apartment the day after the alleged incident, plus a sworn affidavit from an eyewitness present the preceding evening describing her voluntary conduct.
Why counsel is being approached now

The state-law malicious prosecution period under CPLR § 215(3) runs one year from favourable termination and expires in August 2026. The § 1983 claims run three years under McDonough v. Smith and remain open until 2028. The immediate need is a filing against the complaining witness this month; the federal claims against the officers can be developed at a normal pace thereafter.

The prospective client's own position on the District Attorney

Dr. Rahman initially wished to bring malicious prosecution against the District Attorney's office. He has been advised that Imbler v. Pachtman confers absolute immunity on charging and trial advocacy, and he accepts that. He is not asking counsel to plead a barred claim. He is asking whether the coaching visible in the statement video occurred during an investigative phase such that Buckley v. Fitzsimmons and McDonough v. Smith supply a route — and he understands that the answer may be no, in which case the officers remain the appropriate defendants.

Two issues counsel should know at the outset

  • No notice of claim was served within 90 days. General Municipal Law § 50-e relief under subsection (5) would be required for any state-law claim against municipal defendants, within the § 50-i period. The bodycam and statement video are offered as evidence of actual knowledge. The § 1983 claims avoid the issue entirely.
  • A related federal action is pending. Rahman v. United Health Services Hospitals, Inc., No. 3:26-cv-00197 (N.D.N.Y.), before Judge Brindisi and Magistrate Judge Lovric, alleging national-origin discrimination and tortious interference against his former residency program and four physicians. It is at the post-answer stage under a scheduling order entered 10 April 2026 and has been referred to mandatory mediation. Coordination with existing counsel, and care with release language, will be required. Dr. Rahman raises this proactively rather than leaving it to be discovered.

Aggravating features counsel may find material
  • The complaint was filed the day after the complainant's parents discovered the relationship — a documented, datable motive for fabrication.
  • The prosecution ran while the client carried an implanted cardiac defibrillator, placed in February 2024, with a post-operative infection during the same period.
  • The client is an international medical graduate whose immigration status depended on employment that the prosecution made unobtainable — compounding the loss beyond ordinary lost wages.
  • The acquittal was complete. There was no plea, no adjournment in contemplation of dismissal, no reduction. A trial verdict of not guilty.

Why this case merits the call rather than the intake queue

Most malicious prosecution matters turn on inference about the complainant's state of mind. This one has a recording in which someone says the accusation was false and that he was framed, and video of the complainant being coached while giving the statement that caused the arrest. Liability evidence of that character is rare, the acquittal is a clean trial verdict, the economic loss is documented in a signed contract, and the plaintiff is a physician whose career was stopped at its start. The constraint is time, not merit — the state-law period closes this month, and the client is approaching several firms simultaneously for that reason alone.
