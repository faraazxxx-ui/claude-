## FINAL ANSWER

**The malicious prosecution deadline is probably weeks away, not six weeks, and the sources conflict on the date.** Three of four case documents put the acquittal in **August 2025**; one says September 2025. Under CPLR § 215(3) the claim expires one year from favorable termination. Pin the exact verdict date today — if it was August 2025, the window closes this month.

**The amendment is not a tax manoeuvre. It is the master key.** One filing preserves the malicious prosecution claim, creates the § 104(a)(2) physical-injury allocation, raises the mediator's valuation, adds NYSHRL claims that reach the individual defendants personally, and opens the single discovery request that proves everything else. Four payoffs, one document, on the shortest clock in the file.

**And the relationship between the two projects is backwards in every document produced so far.** The venture was treated as the beneficiary of the litigation, gated by it. It is also a *variable inside* it — every dollar the practice earns before settlement offsets the lost-earnings claim, and the records the venture needs are worth more than the marginal dollars the settlement would pay. That inverts both the settlement priorities and the launch sequencing.

## PART 2 — SCQH

**Situation.** Post-answer, pre-discovery. Both defence groups answered on the merits on 9 March 2026 — no Rule 12(b)(6) motion from either. Four causes of action pleaded. Mediation approaching. Damages projected $4.2M–$6.8M. The plaintiff simultaneously needs the settlement as venture capital, the academic records as a licensure precondition, and permanent immigration status that is currently blocked by an unresolved J-1 § 212(e) obligation.

**Complication.** Four things are true at once and pull against each other. (a) The shortest limitation period in the file may expire within weeks, and the case documents disagree about when. (b) The pleaded case does not support the tax treatment the capital plan assumes. (c) The strongest employment-discrimination vehicles may already be time-barred, and the *choice* of Title VI over Title VII suggests counsel already knows it. (d) The defence has adopted a posture that only makes sense as attrition — which means delay is their strategy, and the plaintiff's cash position is the thing being attacked.

**Question.** What single sequence of actions maximises recovery, preserves every live claim, produces the tax character the venture needs, and does not trade away the records the venture cannot launch without?

**Hypothesis.** File one comprehensive amended complaint immediately; issue deposition notices for Ali and the 30(b)(6) designee concurrently; then mediate — from the top of the leverage curve rather than the bottom. Structure the settlement to buy records first and dollars second, with tax allocation language drafted into the release itself.

## PART 4 — PHASE 2: MULTI-ANGLE ANALYSIS

### 4.1 From the defendants' side — the argument they have not yet made

Both groups pleaded failure to state a claim (Ahmed AF 1; UHSH AF 1) and neither moved to dismiss. That defence survives to summary judgment, and there is one version of it that is materially stronger than anything else in either answer.

> **The Title VI employment problem.** 42 U.S.C. § 2000d-3 provides that nothing in the subchapter authorises action "with respect to any employment practice of any employer... except where a primary objective of the Federal financial assistance is to provide employment." UHS's federal funding is Medicare and Medicaid reimbursement — its primary objective is healthcare delivery, not employment. Courts have applied § 2000d-3 to limit private Title VI employment-discrimination claims, and authority is not uniform.
>
> **Assessment: the First Cause of Action is the most vulnerable count in the complaint, and the case-strategy documents rate it "STRONG" without flagging this.** It may well survive — but it should not be the load-bearing discrimination theory. **Counsel must verify current Second Circuit authority on § 2000d-3 before mediation**, because the defence will price this even if it never briefs it.

**The rest of the defence, assessed honestly:**

| Defence | Real strength | Exploitation |
|---|---|---|
| **UHSH AF 6 vs AF 8** — "legitimate business reasons" *and* "outside the scope of employment" | **Mutually exclusive.** Cannot both be true | Contention interrogatory forcing an election. Whichever they abandon, the other side of the case improves |
| **UHSH AF 7** — same-decision / mixed-motive | **Concedes the acts occurred.** Under *Price Waterhouse* the plaintiff need only show discrimination was a motivating factor | Depose to the concession: "You say you would have taken these actions anyway — so the actions were taken?" |
| **Ahmed's Answer ¶ 2** — admits "formerly associated," denies authority or influence | **Discovery gold, and a trap he set for himself.** He must now define "formerly associated" and account for his actual duties | Depose faculty and residents on his real role. Every witness who describes him as teaching faculty converts his denial into a credibility problem |
| **Ahmed AF 4 / AF 5** — personal jurisdiction, improper service | **Weak but curable-adverse.** Tortious acts in Binghamton; minimum contacts plain | Verify service records now and cure any defect rather than litigate it |
| **Both groups' failure-to-mitigate** | **Cuts both ways — see Part 5.3** | Defendants created the conditions preventing mitigation: withheld records, active prosecution |
| **Statute of limitations** | Real as to defamation (NY 1-year); continuing-violation doctrine answers the rest | Last adverse act is post-graduation record obstruction, July 2024 onward |

### 4.2 What the defence posture actually signals

No Rule 12(b)(6) from either group. No counterclaims. No affirmative narrative — neither answer offers a "what really happened" account. Both demand a jury. Ahmed retained separate counsel rather than joining Barclay Damon's representation.

**Read together, this is an attrition posture, not a merits posture.** They did not believe they could win on the pleadings, they are not asserting a competing story, and they have separated Ahmed's personal exposure from the institution's. A defence with facts on its side behaves differently.

**Two consequences.** First, **the plaintiff controls the narrative** — there is no counter-story in the record to displace. Second, **delay is the defence strategy**, and it is aimed at a plaintiff who is unemployed, funding litigation from family transfers, and carrying an immigration clock. Speed is therefore not impatience; it is the counter-strategy.

### 4.3 From the judicial side

A district judge in NDNY sees an acquitted physician, a cardiac patient with an implanted defibrillator alleging he was made to work septic with a PICC line, an audio recording in which someone says "we framed him," and an institution whose two affirmative defences contradict each other. The equities are unusually favourable. **What a court will not tolerate is a plaintiff who missed his own limitation period** — which is why Part 3.1 conflict #1 is an emergency rather than a housekeeping item.

## PART 5 — PHASE 3: SOLUTION ENGINEERING — THE AMENDMENT

### 5.1 Why one filing carries four payoffs

| Payoff | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| **Preserves the malicious prosecution claim** | CPLR § 215(3), one year from favourable termination. Against Sundas Rashid directly; against the institutional defendants via the instigation chain |
| **Creates the § 104(a)(2) tax exclusion** | Under the origin-of-the-claim doctrine, the IRS looks to what the payment replaced. Physical-injury counts must be **pleaded** for the allocation to have a foundation. Currently none are |
| **Raises the settlement number** | A mediator prices the **pleaded** case, not the provable one. Four counts price lower than nine |
| **Unlocks the decisive discovery** | Pleading malicious prosecution and the HIPAA/FERPA breach makes the Sundas-family communications squarely relevant and hard to resist |

### 5.2 What belongs in it

| Add | Basis | Limitation status | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Malicious prosecution** | CPLR § 215(3); *Broughton v. State* elements all satisfied | **EXPIRING — days to weeks** | **1** |
| **NYSHRL, Exec. Law § 296** | National-origin discrimination; **three-year** SOL, **no** exhaustion requirement | Alive to ~July 2027 | **2** |
| **NYSHRL § 296(6) aiding and abetting** | Reaches Ahmed, Nadeem, Rehman and Ali **personally** — Title VI does not | Alive to ~July 2027 | **2** |
| **Physical-injury counts** — negligence / reckless endangerment / IIED with physical manifestation | Brugada aggravation; ICD; forced work while septic on PICC line and IV vancomycin; ICU admission | CPLR § 214 three-year, or § 215(3) for intentional torts — **verify per count** | **1** (tax) |
| **FMLA interference and retaliation** | 29 U.S.C. § 2615. Eligibility satisfied: ~33 months, 1,250+ hours, 50+ employees | Two years, three if wilful — **alive** | **3** |
| **Negligent supervision / retention** | UHS knew of the pattern and did not act | Three years | **3** |
| **ADA / Title VII** | Requires EEOC charge plus right-to-sue letter | **CONDITIONAL — resolve conflict #3 first** | Hold |

> **The strategic point about NYSHRL.** If Title VI's employment limitation (Part 4.1) is real and Title VII is time-barred, **NYSHRL § 296 becomes the primary discrimination vehicle** — three-year SOL, no administrative exhaustion, and individual aider-and-abettor liability that Title VI cannot reach. It is available today and it is a better fit for these facts than the count currently carrying the case. This is the most important addition after malicious prosecution.

### 5.3 The mitigation paradox — and why it resolves cleanly

Both defence groups pleaded failure to mitigate. That defence is defeated by *documented effort*, not by *achieved income*. But achieved income **offsets** lost earnings. So the two pull apart:

- **Document mitigation exhaustively** — every application, every licensure attempt, every credentialing block. This defeats the affirmative defence.
- **Recognise that income arriving before settlement reduces the claim it was meant to fund.**

**These reconcile without any manoeuvring, because the reason he cannot earn is itself the claim.** UHS withheld the records; the records block licensure and credentialing; the prosecution blocked it further. The inability to mitigate is a *consequence of the defendants' conduct* — which is exactly what the Fourth Cause of Action alleges. The honest position and the advantageous position are the same one.

**Scheduling consequence for the venture:** target settlement in **2026**, before the practice reaches meaningful revenue in Q3–Q4 2027. This does not delay the business — Part 2 of the business plan already holds the launch behind gates G1–G3, and those gates depend on the very records the settlement must deliver.

## PART 6 — SETTLEMENT: BUY RECORDS BEFORE DOLLARS

### 6.1 The arithmetic

| | Marginal dollars | Record relief |
|---|---|---|
| **$500,000 additional settlement** | ≈ **$250,000** retained after the ~50% blended federal / NY / NYC / NIIT stack on ordinary income | — |
| **Expungement + good-standing letter + licensing support + neutral-reference covenant** | — | Unblocks NY licensure and credentialing → unblocks the practice (**$6M revenue by Year 6** on the plan's own model) → unblocks the physician NIW, which requires clinical service the records currently prevent |

**The trade is not close.** Marginal cash is taxed at roughly half; the records are the precondition for everything the cash was supposed to build. A plaintiff without a business plan should maximise dollars. **This plaintiff should maximise records and accept a lower number to get them ironclad.**

### 6.2 Non-monetary terms — reordered by value to *this* plaintiff

1. **Complete expungement** of the probation and all adverse academic entries, with **ACGME-compliant replacement documentation** — not merely deletion, but a defensible record.
2. **Affirmative licensing support letter** from the Program Director, in a form pre-agreed and attached as a settlement exhibit.
3. **Neutral-reference covenant** binding UHS and each individual defendant, with a designated single point of contact for all inquiries.
4. **Formal letter of good standing** confirming successful completion of the programme.
5. **Written confirmation of federal funding sources** — needed independently for the Title VI record.
6. **Tax allocation language** drafted into the release itself: claim-by-claim allocation, W-2 versus 1099 designation, and express identification of the physical-injury component.
7. No confidentiality clause extending to the underlying facts.
8. Policy reform on resident due process — lowest priority, highest optics.

> **Item 6 is not a non-monetary term. It is the single largest dollar item in the settlement**, and it is available only while the agreement is still being drafted. Once executed, the tax character is fixed permanently.

### 6.3 The leverage curve

```
LEVERAGE
   ▲
   │                          ╭── PEAK: amendment filed + deposition
   │                        ╭─╯   notices served, before Ali and the
   │                      ╭─╯     30(b)(6) designee actually testify
   │                    ╭─╯
   │      ← you are here
   │    ╭─╯
   │  ╭─╯
   └──┴────────────────────────────────────────────────────► TIME
      Now                Q4 2026              Post-deposition
      (4 counts)         (9 counts +          (leverage spent —
                          notices out)         they know what

```

**Mediating now is mediating at the bottom of the curve.** Four pleaded counts, no depositions noticed, the audio recording not yet deployed in a transcript. Defendants' rational move is to settle *before* Ali testifies and before the recording is authenticated on the record — which means the plaintiff's leverage peaks after the amendment lands and the notices issue, and decays once testimony exists.

**Recommendation:** if mediation is already calendared, get the amendment filed first. If it can be sequenced, notice the Ali and 30(b)(6) depositions before the session. Do not enter the room holding four counts.
