## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The business.** A physician-owned New York primary care practice that sells physician *time* rather than visit volume, priced for working middle-income households, delivered through home visits and sustained remotely, capitalized without interest-bearing debt, paired with a separate U.S. charitable entity that funds care for those who cannot pay at market rates.

**The wedge.** A household earning $120–250K in the New York metro is not short of money — it is short of access. Insurance networks ration primary care by panel availability, not by price. That household is currently receiving worse care than it can afford. It will pay directly for an unhurried hour at home, after work, from a physician who knows its history.

**The three findings that shaped this plan.**

1. **Pure home-visit delivery does not clear physician cost.** Travel destroys billable density. At $250/hour and realistic routing, one physician generates roughly $207K of revenue against a loaded New York internist cost near $290K. **The model as originally conceived loses money per physician.** The fix is in the plan and it comes from the founder's own experience — see *The Correction*, below.
2. **New York's insurance law forces fee-for-service, and Islamic contract law independently agrees.** New York has no direct-primary-care safe harbor. A flat recurring membership fee for undefined future care risks being treated as unauthorized insurance under N.Y. Insurance Law § 1102. The prohibition on *gharar* forbids the same structure for its own reasons. **Price per encounter. Never per period.**
3. **Non-physicians cannot own the clinical entity.** New York's corporate practice of medicine doctrine, tightened again in 2026, requires physician ownership, majority board control, and all voting shares — and bars percentage-of-revenue management fees as fee-splitting. Both non-physician partners must be housed in a separate MSO on a fixed fee.

**The Correction — the actual operating model.** The founder trusts his own physician completely and **has never met him in person**: four years of relationship built entirely by telephone. That is the whole thesis in one data point. **The home visit is the acquisition and trust-formation instrument. Remote continuity is the margin engine.** Home visit to establish the relationship and for periodic hands-on assessment; telephone and video for interval care, where travel time is zero and billable density nearly doubles. This is not a compromise of the model — it is the only version of the model that survives arithmetic, and the founder's own life is the proof of concept.

**Year 1 is not a healthcare business.** It is a legal, tax, immigration, and licensure remediation project with a small clinical pilot attached. Anyone reading this plan expecting Q1 revenue has misread the sequencing. Three hard deadlines inside five months govern everything (see *The Four Clocks*).

A business that loses $83,000 per physician does not scale — it accelerates toward insolvency. **This is why the original conception, however clinically sound, could not have been financed as stated.**

### The fix — hybrid delivery

| Hybrid model | Home visit | Remote (phone/video) |
|---|---|---|
| Share of billable hours | 30% | 70% |
| Rate | $350/hr | $200/hr |
| Travel overhead | High | Zero |
| Function | Establish relationship; hands-on exam; periodic reassessment | Sustain relationship; interval care; titration; results review; family coordination |

| | |
|---|---|
| Blended realized rate | ~$245/hr |
| Billable hours/day (remote-weighted) | ~6.2 |
| **Annual billable hours** | **~1,140** |
| **Revenue per physician FTE** | **~$280,000** |

### Labor strategy by phase — the discipline that makes it work

**Do not hire a salaried physician until panel density supports one.** Fixed physician salary against an unbuilt panel is the standard way small practices die.

| Phase | Labor structure | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Y1 pilot | Founder only, drawing minimally | No fixed clinical salary. Panel building is the work. |
| Y1.5–Y2 | Founder + **1099 moonlighting dual-certified specialists** at $150–175/hr | Marginal-cost capacity, no benefits load, no idle time. Contribution margin $70–95/hr. This is the arbitrage: cardiologists and nephrologists retain IM boards and will sell marginal hours. |
| Y3+ | Convert highest-utilization 1099s to employed as their panels justify it | Salary only once a physician's panel demonstrably covers loaded cost. |
| Throughout | RN / paramedic for logistics; Quest in-home phlebotomy | Never spend physician hours on a task a licensed non-physician can do. Watch NY supervision rules and CPOM. |

### Household economics

| | |
|---|---|
| Typical household utilization | ~6 physician-hours/year |
| Annual revenue per household | ~$1,470 |
| Households per physician FTE | ~190 |
| Target gross margin (Y3+) | 45–50% |

**Compare honestly:** a family of four on a mid-tier employer plan already pays $6,000–9,000 annually in premium share plus deductible for primary care they struggle to access. $1,470 for genuine access is not a luxury purchase. **That is the pitch, and it is defensible on arithmetic rather than sentiment.**

### Q2 — FORMATION (Nov 2026 – Jan 2027)

**Q2 gate:** Entities formed, DFS position obtained, insurance bound, records clean.

### Q3 — PILOT (Feb – Apr 2027)
*First revenue. Deliberately small.*

- **One geography.** A single dense catchment — one borough segment or one Broome/Binghamton-area cluster. **Density is the entire economic argument;** dispersed patients destroy the margin. Do not accept a household outside the route to be accommodating.
- **Target: 40 households.** Not 200. The founder's residency panel took six months to stabilize; there is no reason to think this differs.
- **Acquisition:** physician referral, mosque and community networks, employer introductions via the partners. Zero paid acquisition in the pilot — if the model cannot be sold by word of mouth, paid channels will only buy churn.
- **Instrument everything:** billable hours/day, home-vs-remote mix, travel minutes per encounter, realized rate, household utilization, retention at 90 days, NPS, referral rate.
- **Foundation first cycle:** open the zakat channel to Broome County and NY-metro Muslim physicians. Community needs assessment (the census requirement) executed and documented as a CHNA.

**Q3 gate — the real one.** *Do the instrumented economics match the model?* Specifically: **billable hours/day ≥ 5.5** and **realized blended rate ≥ $230.** If both hold, scale. If billable density is the miss, fix routing and the home/remote mix before adding physicians. If rate is the miss, the market has spoken about price and the plan must be re-cut.

### YEAR 1.5 — SCALE THE CORE (Aug 2027 – Jan 2028)

- 2.0 physician FTE-equivalents; ~200 households.
- MSO builds real capability: centralized scheduling and routing, billing, credentialing, recruiting.
- **Physician recruiting engine** — the actual constraint on growth from here. Dual-certified specialists wanting marginal income, and physicians who left practice over administrative burden. Both are recruitable on the *nature of the work*, which is this venture's cheapest competitive advantage.
- Foundation: first full annual zakat cycle closed, audited, published. Transparency is the product here.
- **Pharmaceutical decision point.** The zero-tariff generics window opened Aug 1, 2026 and closes Aug 1, 2028. If pursuing: NY wholesale distributor license, DSCSA compliance, and sourcing **exclusively** from Indian manufacturers holding current U.S. ANDAs. Treat it as an explicitly time-boxed bridge — the schedule steps to 100% then 200%, and it is an onshoring mandate, not an invitation to build an import business.

### YEAR 2 — MULTI-PHYSICIAN (2028)
*Revenue: ~$500K. Approximately breakeven.*

- 4 geographies, ~340 households, 2 FTE-equivalents.
- Employer channel: small and mid-size employers buying blocks of physician hours as a benefit. **Structure carefully** — an employer-paid arrangement can look like a group health plan and pull in ERISA and ACA implications. Counsel before pilot.
- **Onshore pharmaceutical decision** — the durable version. JV or fill-finish with an Indian ANDA holder onshoring capacity, ahead of the Aug 2028 tariff step. If not proceeding, close the file rather than leaving it open.
- **Cosmetics acquisition** executes here if at all, funded by settlement proceeds, on non-interest-bearing terms (seller cost-plus or equity-only). MoCRA diligence is the gating item.
- **Physician exchange program — architecture correction.** Foreign-trained physicians cannot practice in **New York** without U.S. residency. New York has no alternative IMG pathway. Several states now do: **Florida** (2024 — foreign license, four years recent practice, ECFMG certification, and a Florida position held ≥2 years), **North Carolina** (Internationally-Trained Physician Employee license effective Jan 1, 2026, oriented to rural service), **Illinois**, **Missouri**, **Tennessee**, **Virginia**, and **Washington** (2026 pilot converting supervised practice to full primary care licensure). **Consequence: the exchange program must be a separate geographic entity from the New York practice.** North Carolina's rural-oriented ITPE license is the closest structural match to the model. Arkansas — named in the original dictation — must be verified against the FSMB chart before any commitment. Confirm current status of every pathway with the state board; this area is legislating fast.

### YEAR 3 — DENSITY AND CREDIBILITY (2029)
*Revenue: ~$1.06M. EBITDA 12–15%.*

- 4 FTE-equivalents, ~720 households, 6–8 geographies.
- First employed (non-1099) physicians, converted only where panel economics demonstrably cover loaded cost.
- **Publish the outcomes data.** Continuity-of-care effect on utilization, ED avoidance, and adherence. Two audiences: malpractice underwriters (premium reduction) and New York State (the credible version of the burden-reduction argument). The founder's observation that placebo and presence effects go unstudied in American empirical medicine is correct — and a practice with 700 longitudinal households is positioned to actually study it. **This is a research asset, not a marketing line.** Pair it with an academic partner and an IRB.
- Founder's **NIW service clock** running if that path was selected — five years of HPSA/MUA service. Confirm the venture's structure satisfies USCIS while the cash-pay practice remains staffed by physicians not under service obligation.
- Nursing-home and homebound-elderly program through the Foundation, where the visitation thesis is strongest and the population most neglected.

### YEARS 4–5 — NETWORK (2030–2031)
*Revenue: ~$2.5M by Y5. EBITDA 18–22%.*

## THE ONE-PARAGRAPH VERSION

American primary care is failing on access, not on medicine, and the failure is worst for households that can actually afford to pay. This venture sells unhurried physician time directly to those households — established in the home, sustained by phone — at a price that is a fraction of what they already spend on insurance they cannot effectively use. It is capitalized without interest, structured so non-physicians never control clinical decisions, and paired with a charitable entity that extends the same care to those who cannot pay, funded by the zakat of physicians who have wanted a credible place to send it. It is not a charity and it is not concierge medicine for the wealthy. **It is a wager that the relationship between a physician and a family is the most underpriced asset in American healthcare** — and the founder's own physician, whom he has never met and trusts with his life, is the evidence.
