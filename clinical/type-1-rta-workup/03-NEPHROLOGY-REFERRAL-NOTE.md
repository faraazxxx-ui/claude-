## Reason for referral

## Assessment

**Provisional diagnosis: complete distal (Type 1) renal tubular acidosis.**

Supporting the diagnosis:

1. Normal-anion-gap metabolic acidosis with a bicarbonate of 18 mmol/L.
2. Failure of urinary acidification — urine pH 6.5–7.0 despite systemic acidosis.
3. Nephrolithiasis with flank pain and hematuria, the expected downstream consequence of the alkaline-urine, hypercalciuric, hypocitraturic milieu that distal RTA produces.
4. A urinary tract predisposed to infection, consistent with the observed *Klebsiella* episode.

Arguing against the infection as the primary explanation for the alkaline urine:

- Urease-driven alkalinization characteristically produces a urine pH above 7.5, frequently 8–9; hers is 6.5–7.0, which is more typical of distal RTA.
- The alkaline urine has persisted after microbiological cure.
- **Most importantly, urease activity does not produce a systemic metabolic acidosis.** The bicarbonate of 18 requires a separate explanation.

**Two interpretive caveats I want to flag explicitly, as both bear on how you will want to test her:**

1. **The urine anion gap is not usable in this patient.** Her ketoanions are unmeasured urinary anions excreted with sodium and potassium, which renders the UAG falsely positive and counterfeits the impaired-ammoniagenesis signature of distal RTA. I have therefore requested a **urine osmolal gap** to estimate ammonium directly.
2. **The urine pH requires re-demonstration under clean conditions** — first-morning void, fresh and capped, measured by pH meter rather than dipstick, with a concurrent venous blood gas and a documented sterile culture. The existing value was obtained in the window of a urease-capable organism and is directionally persuasive but not yet definitive.

**The ketonuria** is most plausibly explained by reduced caloric intake over a month of pain and infection, with possible contribution from her high-protein, low-carbohydrate dietary pattern. Diabetic ketoacidosis is effectively excluded by the normal anion gap; β-hCG and glucose/HbA1c are being checked.

## Differential under consideration

| Hypothesis | Rationale |
|---|---|
| **Medullary sponge kidney with distal RTA** | The single diagnosis that accounts for the acidification defect, nephrocalcinosis, stones, hematuria, and infection susceptibility together. CT urography requested. |
| **Sjögren's syndrome with distal RTA ± tubulointerstitial nephritis** | The commonest acquired cause of distal RTA in adult women. Anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB, ANA, RF, quantitative immunoglobulins, SPEP/IFE requested. |
| **Drug-induced (topiramate, acetazolamide, zonisamide)** | An exact and fully reversible phenocopy. Medication reconciliation completed/underway. |
| **Chronic urease-organism infection with struvite lithiasis** | Would explain alkalinity and stones but not the acidosis. Stone composition and test-of-cure culture requested; extended incubation for *Corynebacterium urealyticum* requested. |
| **Plasma cell dyscrasia with acquired tubulopathy** | SPEP/UPEP with immunofixation and serum free light chains requested. |
| **Sickle cell trait** | Papillary necrosis causes hematuria and impaired acidification. Hemoglobin electrophoresis requested. |
| **Independent glomerular disease** (e.g. IgA nephropathy) | Phase-contrast microscopy will fork glomerular from non-glomerular bleeding. |

## Investigations initiated

- **Acid–base:** venous blood gas; CMP with potassium, chloride and albumin; magnesium, phosphate, uric acid; albumin-corrected anion gap
- **Acidification:** first-morning sterile urine pH by meter with concurrent VBG; urine osmolality, sodium, potassium, chloride, urea, glucose for the **urine osmolal gap**
- **Autoimmune:** ANA, anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB, RF, quantitative immunoglobulins, SPEP/UPEP with immunofixation, serum free light chains, C3/C4, ESR/CRP, TSH/anti-TPO, hemoglobin electrophoresis
- **Stone:** two 24-hour urine collections (volume, creatinine, calcium, citrate, oxalate, uric acid, pH, sodium, potassium, magnesium, phosphorus, sulfate, urea nitrogen, ammonium, cystine, supersaturation indices); **stone composition analysis on any calculus recovered** — patient given a strainer
- **Imaging:** non-contrast CT abdomen/pelvis renal stone protocol; **CT urography**; DEXA
- **Hematuria:** phase-contrast microscopy for dysmorphic RBCs and casts; urine protein/creatinine ratio; **parallel Urology referral placed**

## Management initiated pending your review

- Fluid intake targeted to **urine output >2.5 L/day**
- **Potassium citrate** to be initiated once potassium and renal function are confirmed — deliberately a **potassium rather than sodium** salt, since sodium alkali raises urinary calcium
- Titration planned **to serum bicarbonate 22–24 and urinary citrate >320 mg/day rather than to urine pH**, given the risk of aggravating calcium phosphate supersaturation in an already alkaline urine
- Dietary counselling: sodium restriction, moderation of animal protein, **maintenance (not restriction) of dietary calcium taken with meals**, and pasteurized dairy only
